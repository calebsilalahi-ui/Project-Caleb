What is Fasting?

Fasting means choosing not to eat (or eating very little) for a set period of time, then eating again.

It is about WHEN you eat.

16:8 — fast 16 h, eat in 8 h 5:2 — eat less 2 days/week Religious fasting (e.g. Ramadan)
2

What is Calorie Deficit?

A calorie deficit means you take in fewer calories (energy from food) than your body uses each day. It is about HOW MUCH you eat.

Calories INfood & drinks
<
Calories OUTbody + activity
=
Deficitbody uses stored fat

How it works: when food energy is not enough, the body uses its stored energy (fat) to make up the gap — so weight slowly goes down. A small deficit is safer than a big one.

Fasting vs Calorie Deficit

FastingCalorie Deficit
Main focusTiming — when you eatAmount — how much energy you eat
Daily routineLong breaks with no food, then an eating windowRegular meals, just smaller portions or lighter food
Weight loss?Only if it also leads to eating fewer calories overallYes — a deficit is the reason weight goes down
Who should avoidTeens, people with diabetes, eating disorders, pregnancyAnyone going too low (crash diets), growing teens without advice
Key idea: fasting is one way to create a calorie deficit — but it is not the only way, and it is not "magic".

Possible Benefits

  • Healthy weight controlHelps adults with overweight lose weight slowly.
  • Better heart & blood sugarMay improve blood pressure, cholesterol and insulin levels in adults.
  • Stops mindless snackingA clear eating schedule can reduce late-night snacking.
!

Possible Risks

  • Low energy & poor focusTiredness, headaches, dizziness and bad mood ("hangry").
  • Missing nutrients & muscleToo little protein, iron or calcium; the body may lose muscle, not just fat.
  • Unhealthy food habitsOvereating after fasting, or a higher risk of eating disorders.

Myth vs Fact

✕ MYTH
"Skipping meals is the fastest and best way to lose weight."
✓ FACT
Weight loss depends on total calories over time. Skipping meals often leads to overeating later.
✕ MYTH
"The fewer calories I eat, the healthier I am."
✓ FACT
Eating too little slows your metabolism and causes tiredness, weak muscles and missing nutrients.
✕ MYTH
"During fasting hours I can't drink anything."
✓ FACT
In most intermittent fasting plans, water is allowed and staying hydrated is important.
✕ MYTH
"Fasting burns fat in a special way, so calories don't matter."
✓ FACT
Studies show fasting and normal calorie cutting give similar weight loss when total calories are the same.

Teenagers & a Healthy Lifestyle

Teenagers are still growing — bones, muscles, brain and hormones all need enough energy and nutrients every day. Extreme dieting or strict calorie restriction can do real harm:

Slower growthLess bone strength and height gain; possible delayed puberty or missed periods.
Weaker brain powerPoor concentration, memory and school performance; low energy for sport.
Mental health riskDieting in teens is linked to eating disorders and is a risk for future weight gain.
Balanced meals:
½ veggies & fruit, ¼ protein, ¼ grains
Move 60 minutes
every day
Sleep 8–10
hours a night
Drink water,
limit sugary drinks
Golden rule: focus on healthy habits, not a number on the scale. Before trying any diet or fasting plan, talk to a parent, doctor or school nurse.
